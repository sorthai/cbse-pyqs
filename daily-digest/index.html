Aadhaar and voter rolls: the case for identity-based electoral reform

Supreme Court upholds EC's voter roll revision; article advocates Aadhaar-based digital verification for efficient, accurate, and cost-saving electoral lists. The Election Commission (EC) is conducting a Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, which has deleted over 13 crore names since June 2025. The Supreme Court upheld the SIR exercise but clarified that citizenship determination rests with the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) , not the EC . The current SIR method involves document-intensive, house-to-house verification, costing thousands of crores and mobilizing around 10 lakh public servants. The article advocates for an Aadhaar -based alternative for voter list revision, citing its cost-effectiveness and efficiency. Aadhaar is proposed as a tool to verify identity, remove duplicates, confirm age, and cross-check addresses, with linkage remaining voluntary. The SIR process places a significant burden on vulnerable populations like the poor, elderly, migrants, and women due to its reliance on physical documents and compressed deadlines.

National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) is upgrading its 1033 National Highway Helpline with advanced technology. The aim is to provide faster assistance, precise location identification, multilingual support, and improved coordination. The enhancement seeks to significantly improve user safety and overall travel experience on National Highways. Human operators will continue to play a vital role, especially for critical decision-making during emergencies.

Personal guarantor insolvency: proposed IBBI safeguards after the Subhash Chandra case

IBBI proposes four key amendments to personal guarantor insolvency rules, tightening safeguards for creditors after the controversial Subhash Chandra case. The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) has proposed four key amendments to the insolvency resolution process for personal guarantors to corporate debtors. These amendments aim to align safeguards for personal guarantors with those already present in the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) for companies. The proposals follow a controversial National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) single bench order in the Subhash Chandra case , which approved a repayment plan offering creditors a significant haircut of 99.97% on admitted claims of ₹22,006.57 crore. The proposed changes include barring 'related parties' of the guarantor from voting, mandating examination of avoidance transactions, requiring independent asset valuation, and documenting creditors' commercial reasoning.

India and MERCOSUR signed the First Additional Protocol to their Preferential Trade Agreement (PTA) . The Protocol facilitates the acceptance of electronic Certificates of Origin for trade between the parties. This initiative aims to promote paperless trade, modernize customs procedures, and reduce transaction costs. The original India-MERCOSUR PTA was signed in 2004 and offers preferential tariffs on 450 tariff lines by India and 452 by MERCOSUR. The signing marks a significant step towards digitalizing trade documentation and enhancing efficiency in bilateral trade. Electronic Certificates of Origin will hold the same legal validity as paper versions, streamlining verification processes.

Chandrayaan-1 data points to a possibly older lunar impact basin

Chandrayaan-1 data reveals Australe Basin, potentially Moon's oldest impact crater, offering new insights into lunar geology and future missions. Planetary scientists from the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) , Ahmedabad, have confirmed the existence of a previously hidden impact crater, the Australe Basin , on the Moon's southeastern hemisphere. This discovery was made using mineralogical data collected by NASA 's Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M3) instrument aboard India's Chandrayaan-1 mission. The Australe Basin exhibits a unique mineralogical composition, featuring basalts that are relatively lower in calcium and higher in magnesium, and is characterized by 248 small basalt ponds arranged circularly. Researchers suggest that this newly identified basin could be older than the South Pole Aitken Basin , which is currently recognized as the Moon's largest and oldest known impact basin, formed approximately 4.25 to 4.33 billion years ago.

Quantum computing: Indian researchers tackle decoherence in one step

Raman Research Institute scientists develop a novel 'single-shot operation' to combat decoherence and entanglement sudden death, enhancing quantum computer stability and reliability. Scientists at the Quantum Information and Computing (QuIC) laboratory at Raman Research Institute (RRI) in Bengaluru have developed a new method to enhance the stability of quantum states. The technique involves a "single-shot operation" to control decoherence , a phenomenon that causes quantum states to disintegrate. This breakthrough, led by Urbasi Sinha, offers a new control parameter by leveraging the timing of the operation. The research findings were published in the American Physical Society's Physical Review A . Quantum computers utilize unique properties like quantum entanglement and superposition, unlike traditional computers that use 0s and 1s. Quantum states are inherently fragile and prone to rapid disintegration upon interaction with the external environment, known as decoherence. "Entanglement sudden death" is a condition where entanglement vanishes abruptly, even before normal disintegration.

Hindi Diwas: how the Constituent Assembly settled the language question

On Hindi Diwas, revisit the Constituent Assembly's historic Munshi-Ayyangar formula designating Hindi as official language, English as associate, resolving contentious language debate. On September 14, 1949 , the Constituent Assembly adopted the Munshi-Ayyangar formula to resolve India's language question. This formula designated Hindi as the official language of the Union, not a national language. English was permitted to continue for all official purposes for 15 years after the Constitution's commencement. The date September 14 is now celebrated as Hindi Diwas . The provisions of this formula were later incorporated into Articles 343-351 in Part XVII of the Indian Constitution . The debate in the Constituent Assembly involved factions from Hindi-speaking areas advocating for Hindi as the national language and non-Hindi-speaking regions emphasizing unity in diversity.
